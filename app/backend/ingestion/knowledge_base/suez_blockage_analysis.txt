Analysis: Suez Canal Blockage — Asia–Europe Maritime Corridor

Event: A grounded vessel closes the Suez Canal corridor (historical analogue: 2021
Ever Given grounding; scenario pattern reused for operational drills).
Duration: Approximately 6 days of canal closure in the reference incident; diversion
impacts persist for weeks as schedules recover.

Primary Impacts:

All Asia–Europe container and bulk traffic through Suez is halted.

Global trade delay estimates on the order of billions of USD per day while the
canal is closed.

Rerouting around the Cape of Good Hope adds roughly 12–15 days of transit time
and significant extra fuel burn.

European gateway ports such as Rotterdam see delayed inbound Asia cargo and
downstream inventory pressure for automotive and pharmaceutical shipments.

East Coast US ports that rely on Suez-linked Europe–Asia rotations experience
secondary schedule distortion and inventory gaps for EU-linked goods.

Mitigation Strategy:

Multi-source suppliers (for example near-shore or Mediterranean alternatives)
reduce exposure versus single Asia–Europe sea lane dependence.

Safety stock at key European hubs (Rotterdam and peer gateways) buffers the
extra two weeks of Cape diversion.

Carriers and BCOs with pre-planned Cape routings and bunker hedges recover
schedules faster than those scrambling for ad-hoc capacity.

Key Takeaway: The Suez corridor is a single point of failure for Asia–Europe
maritime freight. Canal blockage forces Cape diversions (~14 days), elevates
value-at-risk on high-value cargoes, and stresses European port throughput until
queues clear.
