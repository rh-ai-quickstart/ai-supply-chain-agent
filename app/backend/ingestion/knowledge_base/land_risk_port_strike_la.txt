Analysis: Port Strike at Los Angeles and Long Beach (LA/LGB)

Event: Labor action halts container operations at the Ports of Los Angeles and Long Beach.
Duration: Multi-day to multi-week stoppage depending on bargaining progress.

Primary Impacts:

Container vessel calls at LA/LGB are suspended or held offshore; Asia–US West Coast
inbound cargo cannot discharge.

Inland distribution centers that depend on LA/LGB face inventory shortfalls within
approximately 72 hours for fast-moving SKUs.

Rail and truck transfer points at the ports idle; container dwell times surge as
equipment and chassis cannot clear the terminals.

Reroute options include Oakland and Prince Rupert, with multi-day rail delays and
higher inland freight costs into Chicago, Dallas, and other hubs.

Economic Impact: Lost sales and expedited freight for retailers and manufacturers
tied to Southern California gateways. Spot trucking and intermodal rates on
LA–Chicago and LA–Dallas lanes spike while capacity is diverted to alternate ports.

Secondary Impacts:

Critical backlog forms at inland DCs once vessels finally discharge post-strike.

Last-mile networks absorb delayed containers unevenly, extending e-commerce and
store replenishment lead times by several days.

Shippers with dual West Coast gateways (e.g., Seattle/Tacoma or Canadian gateways
plus LA/LGB) recover faster than single-port dependent networks.

Key Takeaway: LA/LGB is a primary North American import bottleneck. A port strike
immediately stresses inland rail/truck capacity and DC inventory. Pre-negotiated
alternate gateways, safety stock at inland hubs, and flexible intermodal contracts
are the main resilience levers.
